# The GTM Advantage Is Moving

**Source:** Selling Signals · Bianca Caballero · June 11, 2026

## Core Argument

The channels buyers use to discover information are multiplying while brand control over that discovery process is shrinking. Buyers discover ideas through creators, consult AI tools for recommendations, read reviews and comparison articles, and gather peer commentary — all before they talk to a salesperson or visit a vendor website.

The question is no longer whether you're creating content. It's whether you're showing up where buyers actually look for answers.

## Signal 1: LinkedIn Is Betting on B2B Creators

LinkedIn launched its first creator marketplace, giving marketers a dedicated way to identify and partner with creators who have credibility within specific professional audiences. This is a structural signal: trusted individuals are becoming a more important distribution channel in B2B.

Buyers follow practitioners, operators, founders, and subject-matter experts long before they engage with a company's marketing. Companies can pay for reach — credibility is harder to buy. The brands that consistently show up through trusted voices may build an advantage competitors can't easily replicate.

**RevOps implication:** Track creator-led programs as influence sources, not standalone campaigns. Measure whether creator engagement correlates with opportunity creation, progression, or conversion.

## Signal 2: Buyers Are Discovering Vendors Through Sources You Don't Control

A recent study found that ChatGPT cites vendor-owned websites only a small percentage of the time when recommending B2B SaaS solutions. Whether buyers use AI tools, review platforms, analyst reports, or industry publications, the pattern is consistent: decisions are increasingly shaped by third-party sources.

Being easy to find is no longer enough. Buyers need to encounter your brand in the places they already trust.

**The visibility equation has changed:** Your reputation across the broader market may influence discovery as much as your owned content.

**RevOps implication:** Create a process for capturing buyer-reported discovery sources. Add "What helped you build your shortlist?" to discovery calls. Compare pipeline influence from third-party channels against owned channels quarterly.

## Signal 3: Content Volume Is Not a Strategy

Content Marketing Institute's latest research found that many B2B organizations lack clarity around what content success actually means. As discovery fragments, many teams respond by publishing more content across more channels. But without clear purpose, additional content creates more activity to measure — not more pipeline.

The most effective content helps buyers answer questions, reduce uncertainty, and make decisions. Teams that understand that tend to outperform those focused on production volume.

**RevOps implication:** Connect content reporting to opportunity progression and pipeline influence. Separate activity metrics from outcome metrics. Focus reporting on how content contributes to buying decisions, not publishing volume.

## The Structural Shift

For years, B2B GTM focused on driving traffic to channels brands controlled. Buyers now make decisions somewhere else — learning from creators, consulting third-party sources, gathering information long before vendor engagement.

The companies that earn attention in those pre-funnel moments have an advantage: they're showing up where trust is already being built. The companies still optimizing for owned-channel metrics are measuring a funnel that no longer reflects how buyers actually move.
