# The Most Expensive GTM Mistake Is Repeating What Isn't Working

**Source:** Selling Signals · Bianca Caballero · June 3, 2026

## Core Argument

Teams rarely repeat broken motions because they enjoy wasting budget — they do so because the motion used to work, and "used to work" has a powerful hold on revenue teams. Past success makes a strategy feel safer than it actually is. Once a motion becomes familiar, teams confuse comfort with evidence.

> "A GTM motion has expired when it creates activity but not buyer momentum." — Anirudh Mantha, Senior Editor at Mastek

The most expensive GTM mistakes happen when teams optimize outdated motions instead of re-evaluating them.

## Optimization Theater

When performance declines, teams typically respond by:
- Testing a new subject line
- Adjusting a CTA
- Refreshing a landing page
- Tweaking audience targeting
- Adding more calls, emails, follow-ups

These changes may improve efficiency at the margins but they don't fix a motion that no longer aligns with buyer priorities. This is **optimization theater** — it feels productive because changes are being made, but the team is optimizing around a weak premise.

> "You can't optimize what's not working — even if it worked in the past." — Chris Selland, CEO of Differential Factor LLC

## When to Optimize vs. When to Reassess

**A motion deserves optimization when:**
- The audience is still right
- The problem is still relevant
- Buyers are still moving toward decisions

**A motion needs reassessment when:**
- Buyer behavior has changed
- The same objections keep recurring despite strong execution
- Activity is high but qualified conversations are not

## Diagnosis Framework

Separate execution issues from positioning issues before increasing activity.

**Signals that the motion is the problem (not execution):**
- Same objections recurring regardless of how well reps handle them: "don't understand the value," "can't justify price," "need to think about it," "comparing to lower-value alternative"
- Activity increases but learning does not — more dashboards, busier people, no new market insight
- Declining response rates despite consistent or increased volume

**If the motion is being executed well and still creates weak conversations, stop polishing the motion and revisit the strategy.**

## Practical Audit Steps

**Marketing:**
- Audit top-performing campaigns from six months ago vs. current buyer engagement
- Review recent win/loss conversations for changes in buyer priorities, language, and objections
- Retire campaigns generating activity but not qualified conversations
- Test messaging assumptions before testing creative variations

**Sales:**
- Identify one sales motion that has underperformed for two consecutive quarters
- Separate execution issues from positioning issues before increasing activity
- Review recurring objections — do they point to messaging, audience, or offer problems?
- Evaluate conversation quality and buyer progression, not just activity volume

## The Structural Trap

Repetition often looks responsible from the outside:
- A team running the same campaign looks **consistent**
- A sales org using the same process looks **disciplined**
- Leadership reviewing the same metrics looks **structured**

But structure does not guarantee learning, and consistency does not guarantee progress.

> "If a team is doing more outreach, producing more content, running more ads — but not learning anything new about the market — that is usually a sign the motion needs to change. Optimization should produce sharper insight." — Joshua Maddux, Founder & CEO at 95Visual

## The Right Question

When a familiar GTM play underperforms, don't start with:
**"How do we improve this?"**

Start with:
**"Is this still the right thing to improve?"**

That question can save a lot of budget, meetings, and very sincere post-campaign retrospectives.
