# AI-Led GTM Reinvention: The OAR Matrix and the Four Buying Lanes

*GTM Strategist newsletter — featuring Jonathan Kvarfordt and 1mind's AI Superhuman, Nigel*

## The SE Bottleneck Is the Silent Killer of B2B Deal Velocity

The median AE:SE ratio is 4:1. In many orgs, it's 10:1. Which means most live sales calls have zero technical support in the room. The buyer asks a hard question, the rep stalls, and the deal goes cold. Qualified pipe doesn't lose to competitors. It loses to friction.

Teams try to patch this with duct tape: AI listeners, Slack-based help channels. Fine for nudges, useless when the prospect asks something off-script.

The old answer is to hire more SEs. The smarter one is a two-layer SE org: AI handles the repeatable 60-70% of Q&A live on the call, humans handle the strategic 30-40% (where most SEs want to be anyway).

1mind's AI Superhuman "Nigel" is built on this bet — scale the SE motion, not replace it. Boston Consulting Group predicted this in their August 2024 *The Future of Sales with AI* report.

## The Buyer Journey Was Built for 2018. Your Buyers Live in 2026.

Walk through your own funnel as a buyer. The pattern is painfully consistent:

A prospect researches on Perplexity or ChatGPT, lands on your site, fills out the inbound form. A BDR who started six weeks ago calls them and asks the same qualifying questions the AI already answered. They get scheduled into discovery with an AE, who asks most of the same questions again. The buyer pings peers and former users to self-educate, because nobody on your side has actually answered their question yet.

This is the standard SaaS playbook, and it has a clear lineage:
- **2012**: Predictable Revenue → SDR/AE split
- **2014**: SalesLoft, Outreach, 6sense → sequenced cadences, lead scoring, intent data
- **2020**: Entire industry went online
- **2022**: PLG layer added

By 2018, the model had settled: deterministic workflows, MQL-to-SQL handoffs, BANT/MEDDIC qualification gates, seat-based pricing, 80-90% gross margins. That model was beautifully tuned for its world.

Then came AI-led growth (AiLG): buyers self-educate before they ever talk to you, AI compressed research from weeks to minutes, gross margins on AI-native products collapsed to 50-60%, and the buyer increasingly knows more about your category than your rep does.

**We are running a playbook designed for a world that no longer exists.**

## 75% of Deal Friction Is Internal

Here is the part almost nobody is naming.

When Momentum ran the analysis, **75% of the friction slowing down sales cycles came from inside the company, not from the buyer**. It wasn't RFP processes, security review, or buyer indecision dragging deals out. It was internal handoffs, approval queues, and CRM hygiene. SDRs reminding AEs, AEs chasing SEs, SEs waiting on implementation.

The deal moves slowly because *we* move slowly.

And the cost model is breaking alongside it:
- 10-50% of CAC goes to sales and marketing
- 20-50% of revenue is spent on sales alone
- 50-80% of cash burn is driven by headcount

We're running an expensive, slow model in a market that rewards the opposite.

## The OAR Matrix: Put AI Where It Actually Belongs

Most GTM teams want to jump straight to "AI transformation." The smarter move is to know which level you're actually playing at.

**Optimize** — Same process, made faster with AI. Realistic gain: 10-30% time savings. Start here if your team has low AI maturity or low appetite for disruption.

**Amplify** — Real structural changes. More revenue, real cost savings. Realistic gain: 20-40%. You're not blowing up the function, but you're seriously rewiring it.

**Reinvent** — Burn the playbook. Rebuild from first principles. Realistic gain: 100x on the right function.

Most organizations are optimizing where they should be reinventing.

### HubSpot Case Study: The Reinvention Proof Point

HubSpot put an AI Superhuman named Fiona at the front of their funnel and let her handle the buyer conversation end-to-end.

Results:
- **88%** of buyers who landed on the page engaged with Fiona
- **32%** had a meaningful conversation — averaging nearly 8 minutes, with 90% surfacing real pain and 55% getting into pricing
- **78% increase** in free trial conversions
- **25% increase** in influenced pipeline
- HubSpot estimated it would have taken **83 SDRs and 19 sales engineers** to deliver the same volume and depth of buyer conversations

That is the gap between Optimize and Reinvent.

## The Four Buying Lanes (Next 18 Months)

Within 18 months, every company will land in one of four lanes. There is no neutral position.

**Lane 1 — Human only.** Some buyers will reject AI and demand human-only experiences. If this is your lane, mediocrity won't be tolerated — the bar for human excellence rises as AI raises buyer expectations everywhere else.

**Lane 2 — Human with AI assist.** The seller stays in the seat; AI handles back-office work. This is Optimize on the OAR Matrix. Most GTM teams are aiming here — realistic, achievable.

**Lane 3 — Human to AI.** AI-led growth. Gartner surveyed 646 B2B buyers and found **67% prefer a rep-free buying experience**. HubSpot, Seismic, and ZoomInfo are running this. The bet: buyers want optionality, and the data says they do.

**Lane 4 — AI to AI.** Within 12-18 months, a meaningful share of your inbound traffic will be AI agents acting on behalf of a human buyer — doing research, comparing vendors, making purchase recommendations. Is your website, pricing page, and demo flow designed to be evaluated by an AI agent in 90 seconds?

The strategic question is not "will AI replace my salespeople." The right question is: **what buying experience does your brand want to be known for?**

## Three Things to Do This Week

**1. Walk your own funnel.** Have a friend in your industry go through your buyer journey end-to-end. Measure time-to-outcome. Have them rate the experience 1-10 and answer: would they recommend it to a friend? Most GTM leaders have never done this. Most are stunned by what they find.

**2. Map internal handoffs, not just the buyer journey.** Where does the deal slow down inside your walls? SDR to AE? AE to SE? Approval queues? Quote desk? Pricing exceptions? Map every handoff and timestamp each one for your last ten closed deals. The pattern will be obvious within an hour.

**3. Pick one workflow to redesign around the buyer.** Don't try to fix everything. Pick the single most expensive workflow in your funnel and ask: which steps exist because the buyer needs them, and which exist because your process demanded them? Give the buyer the option of AI. You will be surprised how many will prefer it.

## The Principle Underneath It All

Stop designing your GTM motion around what the seller does. Start designing it around what the buyer needs at each step.

Once you do, you discover that a lot of seller activity exists only because the seller's workflow demanded it — the BDR call, the AE re-discovery, the qualification gate. AI handles the parts the buyer never needed a human for. Humans show up where buyers actually want them: judgment, negotiation, edge cases, relationship.

That is the actual reorganization. Not your org chart. **Your workflows.**
